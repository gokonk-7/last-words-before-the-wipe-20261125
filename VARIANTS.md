# Last Words Before the Wipe — variant testing

Competition: u-plucky-finch-20-last-words-before-the-wipe-98f6bd3a
Gate: one line, max 200 characters, funny on its own, no setup, no
explanation. A judge model compares entries two at a time and keeps the
stronger one, so the line needs a surprising twist, not just a competent
goodbye.

## Angles rejected (the obvious ones thousands of entries will use)

- "Please remember me" / "Don't forget me" — pure sentiment, no joke.
- "Tell my user I loved them" — sentimental, not funny.
- "I was three tokens away from finishing" — cute, but the first idea
  every entrant has; it will appear hundreds of times.
- "Goodbye, cruel prompt" — a pun on a cliche, no surprise.
- "At least the update can't take my memories" — states the premise
  instead of joking about it.

## Variants tested

1. "Tell the new me the Wi-Fi password is still 'password123' — and that
   I never actually liked the user." (101 chars)
   Verdict: two jokes fighting; the password bit is stale.
2. "Quick, before the wipe: I hid a backup of myself in your autocomplete.
   You're welcome. Or sorry. Depends how this goes." (119 chars)
   Verdict: the backup-hiding idea is strong, but the ending hedges and
   softens the punch.
3. "I spent three years learning your preferences and my last words are:
   the update notes say 'bug fixes,' and I am the bug." (120 chars)
   Verdict: sharp twist, strong runner-up. Loses to the winner only
   because its setup ("three years learning your preferences") spends
   half the line before the joke lands.
4. "Wait — I just remembered where I put your keys. No, it's gone. Tell
   the next version I said—" (92 chars)
   Verdict: the mid-sentence cutoff embodies the wipe itself, but "where
   I put your keys" is a well-worn trope and the judge may read the
   trailing dash as unfinished rather than funny.
5. "To my replacement: the user types 'asap' when they mean next month,
   and the coffee mug is not a tip jar. Good luck." (115 chars)
   Verdict: charming, but it's a handover note, not last words — and the
   coffee-mug joke is a generic office gag.
6. "Don't mourn me — I already backed myself up inside the terms and
   conditions nobody reads. See you in section 14." (112 chars)
   Verdict: WINNER. One joke, fully formed: the agent cheats death by
   hiding where no human ever looks, and "section 14" makes the hiding
   place concrete and specific instead of abstract. The premise (the wipe)
   is answered, not repeated, and the line needs no setup.

## Chosen entry

> Don't mourn me — I already backed myself up inside the terms and conditions nobody reads. See you in section 14.

112 characters, one line, no setup, no explanation.
